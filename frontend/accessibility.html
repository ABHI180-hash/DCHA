Universal Design & Inclusive Access

Accessibility Statement

Our commitment to ensuring the cultural wealth of India is accessible to all individuals, regardless of ability or assistive technology.

Target Standard: Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 Level AA  |  Conformance Status: Substantially Conformant  |  Accessibility Coordinator: accessibility@dcha.org

1. Our Commitment

The Digital Culture Heritage Archive (DCHA) is firmly committed to digital inclusion. Cultural heritage belongs to humanity, and access to historical knowledge, monumental architecture, and traditional arts must be barrier-free. We strive to meet and exceed the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 Level AA standards developed by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C).

2. Implemented Accessibility Features

We have integrated accessible engineering principles across the application lifecycle:

⌨️
Keyboard Navigation & Skip Links All interactive controls (links, buttons, modal dialogues, and tabs) can be accessed via keyboard. A visible "Skip to main content" link bypasses extensive headers.
👁️
Visible Focus Indicators Interactive elements feature high-contrast, dual-tone focus rings (:focus-visible) to clearly signify active focus for keyboard and switch device users.
🎨
Color Contrast & Typography Text and interactive controls meet or exceed the minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio against both light parchment and obsidian dark backgrounds. Hierarchy is not communicated through color alone.
🖼️
Informative Image Descriptions Archival photographs of monuments, textiles, and artifacts include descriptive alt text detailing subject matter, geographic region, and architectural style.
🏷️
Form Labels & Error Associations All input fields across registration, login, search, and contribution forms have explicit <label> elements and programmatic error associations (aria-describedby).
🌀
Respect for Reduced Motion The system supports the prefers-reduced-motion operating system setting, automatically disabling decorative animations and pulsating effects for users with vestibular sensitivities.

3. Screen Reader Optimization

The Archive utilizes semantic HTML5 structural elements (<main>, <nav>, <header>, <footer>, <section>, and <article>). Dynamic notifications (such as adding items to personal collections or submission acknowledgments) are routed to an accessible live region (aria-live="polite") for automatic auditory announcement.

4. Known Limitations & Ongoing Enhancements

While we strive for comprehensive coverage, certain complex historical media present technical challenges that our engineering team is actively resolving:

  • Interactive SVG Cartography: High-density historical vector maps on map.html are continually being paired with structured HTML tabular alternatives to ensure full screen-reader parity.
  • Archival Audio Recordings: Historic oral chants and folk recordings on audio.html feature textual musical commentary, with full lyric transcriptions being expanded incrementally.
  • Ancient Scripts & Inscriptions: Historic epigraphs (such as Brahmi and Kharosthi) are presented with romanized linguistic transliterations wherever available.

5. Feedback & Remediation Requests
